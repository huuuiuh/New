The first floor had gone too easily, and Do-yoon did not trust easy.

“Two hours,” Mu-jin said. “Either we’re good or the dungeon is bored.”

They took the stairs. The fog on the second floor came up to their knees and then their waists, and somewhere in it a beast was waiting, and Do-yoon knew exactly which pillar it would come from and could not say so.

It came from the wrong one.

The tendril took him across the shoulder before he saw it, and the world went white, and when he could see again he was on the stone with Mu-jin standing over him and something warm running down his side that was not fog.

Do-yoon’s left arm was severed.

He looked at it for a while. It did not seem to belong to anyone.

“Don’t look,” Mu-jin said. “Kid. Don’t look at it. Look at me.”
