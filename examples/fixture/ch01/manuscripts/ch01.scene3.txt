Outside, the Mapo sky was the color of a wet sidewalk.

Do-yoon stood on the steps of the measurement hall and let the cold get into his jacket. It was exactly same feeling with ten years ago. That was the point. He needed to feel it and not flinch.

He took out his phone. The date on the lock screen was the date he expected. Day zero.

He opened a blank note and typed three lines.

*1. The gate run. Do not let him take the left corridor.*

*2. The compass.*

*3. The Chairman.*

He stared at the third line for a while. Then he deleted it. Some things were safer as memory than as text, even on a phone nobody would ever search.

Two lines. That was enough to start with.

A bus hissed past. A girl on the steps below him was crying into her sleeve, an F-card crushed in her fist. Ten years ago he had done the same thing, three steps to the left. He had cried for an hour and then gone home and not come back for two weeks.

He walked down past her. He did not say anything. There was nothing anyone could have said to him, either.

At the bottom of the steps his phone buzzed.

Park Mu-jin: Tomorrow. 6 a.m. Gate 3, Mapo. Don’t be late, kid.

Do-yoon read it twice.

Last time, Mu-jin had sent the same message. The same words, the same time. Last time, Do-yoon had been late, and Mu-jin had gone in without a porter, and had come out on a stretcher with the left leg of his trousers cut away.

He typed back one word.

*Understood.*

Then he put the phone in his pocket and started walking toward the station, toward a room he had not seen in ten years, toward a morning he was going to spend making sure an old man did not die for him a second time.

The first thing on the list was tomorrow.
